# SAMI — protection and market next steps
## Working founder checklist — not legal advice

The objective is to preserve options before broad disclosure while moving rapidly toward a controlled commercial pilot.

## Immediate — before public launch
1. Preserve a dated invention disclosure describing the technical mechanisms, data architecture, voice/map interaction, source-verification logic, contribution/provenance model, automated planning concepts and export/integration architecture.
2. Keep a dated archive of source code, screenshots, product requirements, diagrams and change history.
3. Obtain a UK patent-attorney review focused on the *technical mechanisms*, not a broad claim to “a site planning app”. Ask for patentability/prior-art advice before disclosing potentially novel implementation details publicly.
4. Obtain an IP/employment-contract review before using employer resources, confidential project data or employer-owned systems/contacts in a manner that could create an ownership dispute.
5. Complete professional UK/international trade-mark clearance before committing commercially to SAMI. SAMI remains a working brand until cleared.
6. Put written confidentiality and IP-assignment terms in place for every external developer, designer or contractor. The operating company should own commissioned code/assets and pre-existing/background IP should be expressly identified.

## Product protection by architecture
Build several layers of defensibility rather than relying on the idea alone:
- Technical IP: patentable technical mechanisms where genuinely novel.
- Copyright: code, UI assets, documentation and original content.
- Brand: cleared trade marks and consistent identity.
- Data: verified, permissioned project intelligence with provenance and history.
- Network effects: trusted field contributions and validation workflow.
- Integrations: enterprise workflows, GIS/CAD, approved datasets and customer systems.
- Operational know-how: construction-specific planning logic and workflows.

## Controlled pilot path
- Select 2–3 design-partner organisations/projects.
- Use NDAs/pilot agreements where appropriate.
- Define a narrow pilot success metric: plan-production time, information-search time, number of systems replaced, constraints found before mobilisation, revision time and user adoption.
- Do not promise unavailable utility/source data. Separate connected authoritative layers from manual observations.
- Preserve SAMI background IP in customer contracts; customers receive agreed use/licence rights rather than ownership of the core platform.

## Product build sequence
**P0 — current prototype**: mapping, site capture, routes, measurements, notes/photos, manual services/constraints, Ask SAMI commands, trusted-source routing, DXF/GIS exports.

**P1 — pilot-ready**: secure accounts, cloud projects, revisions, role permissions, audit history, photo/document storage, import/export, polished plan output, reliable mobile/tablet UX.

**P2 — intelligence**: server-side grounded retrieval, official-source citations, organisation knowledge base, safe action execution, provenance/confidence UI.

**P3 — data platform**: licensed utility/environmental/asset layers, verified community/field observations, moderation and reconciliation, enterprise APIs.

**P4 — automation**: constraint detection, route/site-layout assistance, change detection, conflict alerts, planning recommendations and project reporting — subject to technical validation and IP review.

## Evidence to collect during every pilot
Record baseline vs SAMI time, user role, task completed, errors avoided, information sources consulted, drawing/export destination, constraints identified, revisions handled, safety/escalation events, and qualitative feedback. This becomes the ROI model and future enterprise sales evidence.
